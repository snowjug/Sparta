# Phase 2: controlled test results

**These are controlled tests, not real calls.** Every journey below ran through Sparta's own Twilio adapter, a real
WebSocket carrying mu-law frames, the audio pipeline, the call service and the deterministic safety engine, with
**scripted speech recognition, scripted speech synthesis and a fake Twilio REST API**. No Twilio account, no Azure Speech
resource, no Azure OpenAI deployment and no telephone network were involved. They show that the pipeline Sparta controls
behaves as designed when its external services behave as scripted; they say nothing about how those services behave.

Source: `services/api/tests/api/test_phase2_matrix.py` (12 tests, all passing at commit `53b2406`).
Sanitised evidence: [evidence/phase2-controlled-matrix.json](evidence/phase2-controlled-matrix.json). Re-create it with:

```powershell
$env:SPARTA_WRITE_EVIDENCE = "docs/evidence/phase2-controlled-matrix.json"
python -m pytest tests/api/test_phase2_matrix.py
```

The call ids are generated by the test run; the provider correlation id is a fake `CallSid`; the caller lines are
invented test sentences (no real person, number or recording is involved), and the evidence file holds no transcript.

## Latency (local processing only)

Measured over the 12 tests above with **zero-latency fakes** for recognition and synthesis and **no language model**.
They are Sparta's own processing times on one developer machine, with audio sent faster than real time. They exclude the
500 ms silence the voice-activity detector waits for before it declares speech ended, and every network and service
delay. **They are not what a real call would measure, and no real-call latency has been measured.**

| Quantity | Samples | p50 (ms) | p95 (ms) | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Final transcript latency (`stt_ms`) | 11 | 0.0 | 0.0 | The scripted recogniser answers instantly: this says nothing about Azure Speech |
| Deterministic engine decision (`policy_ms`) | 11 | 21.7 | 68.1 | The only stage here that is real work |
| Model decision (`model_ms`) | 1 | 0.2 | 0.2 | Only the scripted failing model; the real model is unmeasured |
| TTS first audio (Twilio adapter) | 24 | 0.0 | 0.0 | Scripted synthesiser: unmeasured for Azure |
| Service turn total (`turn_total`) | 11 | 40.4 | 88.3 | Caller turn recognised to assistant turn decided |
| Caller's last frame to first assistant audio frame | 8 | 92.6 | 204.5 | Seen by the test's WebSocket client |
| Interruption stop (`clear` sent) | 1 | 1.1 | 1.1 | One sample |

What a real call adds (all **UNMEASURED**): the 500 ms end-of-speech hangover, Azure Speech final latency, the optional
model call, text-to-speech first-byte latency, Twilio's and the carrier's network delay, and Twilio's playback buffer.
The metric names a real deployment exposes are `sparta_stage_latency_ms` (`turn_total`, `stt_ms`, `language_detect_ms`,
`policy_ms`, `model_ms`, `interruption`) and `sparta_provider_latency_ms` (`tts_first_audio`, `stt_service`); the call's
diagnostic report (`GET /api/v1/calls/{id}/report`) gives p50 and p95 per stage for one call. Fill in this table from a
real call before quoting a latency anywhere.

## What these tests do not show

* That Twilio accepts the TwiML, the stream parameters or the signature of the media upgrade, or that its mark and clear
  semantics match the documentation.
* That Azure Speech recognises Hindi, Kannada or code-switched telephone audio, at what accuracy, or with what delay.
* That a real model, or a real voice, behaves as the scripted ones do.
* That a call transferred with `<Dial>` rings the owner's phone and bridges the two parties.
* Anything on an Android phone.
